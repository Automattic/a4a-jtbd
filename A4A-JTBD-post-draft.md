# A living Jobs to be Done map for Automattic for Agencies

I recently took part in a Designomattic Design Den focused on Jobs to be Done. It was a hands on class where we learned the JTBD method and put it to work on a real product challenge, framing each customer need as a job with a clear situation, motivation, and outcome. I came out of it with a way of thinking that fit Automattic for Agencies perfectly, and I wanted to apply it to our whole experience while it was fresh.

This post shares where that work stands today, and where it goes next.

Automattic for Agencies is a relationship tool. It helps agencies earn passive income, manage hundreds or thousands of client sites from one place, and grow their business across the five units we support, including WordPress.com and WooCommerce. The whole program is built to help agencies move from project based work toward recurring revenue, with a commission based tiering system that rewards agencies as their revenue and payment volume climb.

To make that experience easier to reason about, I mapped the full product into a single living JTBD framework. It holds 251 jobs today, laid out as a FigJam style interactive map you can pan, zoom, search, and filter by pillar, status, or page. I also added a search that highlights the jobs tied to a term inside a pillar and dims the rest, so we can zero in on a topic in seconds.

The core idea is simple. We can now see every touchpoint and every job our customers do in the product as it stands today. From there we can validate and streamline those jobs with data and first hand evidence. Every feature release and every CTA we add creates another job, so the map grows alongside the product and stays current.

## Why this helps our customers and our team

For customers, a clear job map points us to the moments that matter so we can simplify their path and take friction out of the way. For our team, it gives us one shared picture of the product, a fast way to spot overlapping or muddy areas, and a backlog grounded in real customer goals. The migration landing page is a good example. It currently serves two pillars at once, and mapping it shows us how to give each page a single clear job.

## The three levels

- **Pillars:** the three high level jobs an agency hires us for. Earn, Build, and Grow.
- **Major functions:** the product areas under each pillar, such as client management, referrals, and partner directories.
- **Micro functions:** the granular tasks inside the UI, each one tied to a specific call to action.

## The job states

We tag each job so the map reflects how the product actually behaves:

- **Draft and Validated:** where a job sits on its path to being backed by evidence.
- **New user and Returning:** jobs that belong to the first run experience and jobs for the steady state.
- **Upcoming:** jobs for features on the way, like our June releases and the Multi-Cloud Platform.
- **Intermittent:** jobs whose availability comes and goes, like seasonal incentives.
- **Redundant:** jobs that overlap across areas and are candidates for consolidation.
- **Tier-gated:** jobs available only to certain agency segments, such as Agency, Pro, and Premier.

## What this unlocks

With every job visible, we can move each one from draft to validated using evidence. We can study the volume of CTAs behind a task to find where the real opportunities and bottlenecks live. We can map jobs to product data to track conversion, and when a job shows low adoption we can ask whether it points to an unmet need before we ever consider removing it. Mapping redundant jobs also lets us streamline the information architecture so each page targets one clear goal.

## Next steps

- **Move to GitHub:** migrate the framework into a repo so it becomes a living document the whole team can access and update through pull requests.
- **Sync to the product:** keep the map in step with every release, so a new feature or CTA shows up as a new job the moment it ships.
- **Build the evidence layer:** back each job with data and customer quotes. Our sources include roughly 15,000 Zendesk tickets from the past two years, partner manager feedback logged in HubSpot, and contextual in product feedback captured right after a user finishes a task.
- **Adopt Outcome Driven Innovation:** rank jobs on a 1 to 5 scale for importance and satisfaction so we can tell table stakes apart from real differentiators.
- **Add primary research:** run customer interviews to surface the innate jobs that rarely show up in feedback channels.
- **Shift to segment language:** frame gated jobs by agency segment, Agency, Pro, and Premier, to give the team sharper clarity on who each job serves.
